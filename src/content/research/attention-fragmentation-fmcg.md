Drawn from my M.Com dissertation, *Discerning the Role of Attention
Fragmentation and Selective Attention in Shaping Consumer Purchase Decisions:
The Case of FMCG Brands in Kolkata*.

It asks a narrow version of a large question: when a shopper's attention is
split across more messages than it can hold, what happens to the decision they
end up making?

The study treats attention as three separable things rather than one —
**attention fragmentation** (how far attention is divided across competing
stimuli), **selective attention** (what a consumer filters toward), and
**information overload** (the point at which more information stops helping).
It tests how each relates to brand trust, and how brand trust in turn relates
to decision quality.

## Method

Survey data from urban FMCG consumers in Kolkata, analysed in SPSS. Brand trust
is modelled as a **mediator** rather than an outcome — treating trust as the
thing attention acts *through*, not the thing it acts *on*.

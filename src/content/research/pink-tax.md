The "pink tax" is the observation that products marketed to women often cost
more than near-identical products marketed to men. This paper took that claim
seriously enough to check it, using secondary data on product pricing,
marketing and packaging across comparable product pairs.

The exploratory framing was deliberate. Before arguing about whether gendered
pricing is discrimination or segmentation, it is worth establishing carefully
where the gap actually appears and where it does not — packaging and marketing
spend explain part of it, and the residual is the part worth arguing about.

My first piece of independent research. Awarded a Certificate of Appreciation
